How the numbers are measured

This month = change from the last close of the previous month to the latest close. "Falling since high" counts trading days since the 52-week closing high. "Last up week" is the most recent completed week (Friday close) that finished above the week before; the current week is shown separately because it isn't finished. Down-week streak = consecutive completed down weeks. The weekly chart runs oldest to newest, with gains above the line and losses below; the dashed last bar is the week still in progress.

Where the reasons come from

Reasons come from news coverage around each stock's biggest down days this month. High confidence = a specific, reported catalyst. Medium = a sector or theme move with supporting coverage. Low = no single catalyst found.

Winners and volatility

Winners = the 50 S&P 500 members with the biggest gain this month, measured the same way. Volatility = how much a stock's daily price swings, as the annualized standard deviation of daily returns over the last 30 trading days (1-year volatility uses the past year). A stock at 60% swings roughly three times as much as one at 20%. Average daily move is the typical size of one day's move, up or down. Beta compares a stock's daily moves with the S&P 500's over the past year: 1.0 moves with the market, 2.0 moves about twice as much.

Sell-a-put numbers

Sell-a-put numbers use the listed put about 37 days out whose delta is nearest 0.30 (roughly a 70% chance of expiring worthless, by the options market's own pricing). Premium = mid of bid/ask, or the last trade when there's no live quote. Annualized = premium ÷ strike × 365 ÷ days to expiry (the return on cash set aside to buy the shares). IV is the at-the-money implied volatility, which we work out from the option prices. "× realized" compares it with the stock's actual 30-day volatility: above 1 means options are pricing in bigger swings than the stock has been making. IV rank shows where today's IV sits in its own range, and only appears after 20 days of history. All options data is from the close and updates once a day.

Pick pattern

Pick pattern scores each falling knife on the 4 traits the best knives shared in a backtest of every monthly list from Sep 2021 to Aug 2026 (using the real S&P 500 members each month): a tech or semiconductor company; falling in a group sell-off (its sector's median stock or the S&P 500 down 3%+ this month); moving with its sector rather than blowing up on its own news (within 12 points of the sector median); and already 30%+ below its 52-week high. Over the next 12 months, knives scoring 4 of 4 averaged +56% (75% beat the S&P 500); 3 of 4 averaged +16%; 0–1 averaged about +3%. The tech result partly reflects the AI boom in those years and may not repeat, and in 2024–25 the higher scores' edge came mostly from a few big winners. Monthly basket: every knife scoring 3 or 4 at the month's last close, equal dollars in each; the top of the basket (highest score, then biggest drop) is also tracked as a single-stock version. Over 10 years (2016–2026) the basket returned about 25% a year vs about 15% for the S&P 500 with the same dollars, including about 23.5% a year in 2016–21, years the rule was not built on. Months before the site existed come from that backtest; later months are tracked live. A higher score means better odds, not a guaranteed pick.

Bottoming signs

Bottoming signs are four simple checks: back above the 50-day average after being below it in the last two weeks; RSI (14-day momentum) up 10+ points from an oversold reading under 30 in the last two weeks; the first up week after two or more down weeks; and a higher low (the last two weeks' low is above a 3-month low set 2–6 weeks ago). Two or more = "Rebounding". They are not predictions and fail often.

Track record

Track record ranks every S&P 500 member by its return in each calendar month and follows the 50 worst (and 50 best) from that month's last close. Months marked Live use the list exactly as this site published it. Earlier months are rebuilt from price history using today's members, so companies that were later dropped from the index are missing, which makes the results look somewhat better than they really were.

Blue chips

Blue chips = the 50 largest S&P 500 companies by market cap (one share class per company). Analyst upside = average price target vs. last close.

Data and live prices

Yahoo Finance daily closes (split-adjusted) for all 503 S&P 500 members, plus Yahoo quote data for valuation and analyst targets. Rankings and reasons refresh every weekday after the close (about 5:30pm ET). Prices on the cards, the blue-chip tiles and the S&P figure update live from Finnhub during market hours, roughly every minute per stock (faster for the stocks on your screen); % this month, % off the high and this week's bar recalculate from the live price.
